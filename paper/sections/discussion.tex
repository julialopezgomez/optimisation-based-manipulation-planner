\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Validating where it matters}
The weakest link is not the search but the sets it searches over
(Section~\ref{sec:results:fidelity}). A dense check is not a certificate;
certifying the thin transition sets with C-IRIS~\cite{dai2024certified}, or
seeding the decomposition on the grasp set, would strengthen the guarantees
without changing the search.

\paragraph{Constructing grasp sets}
We take two ideas from NLP sampling~\cite{toussaint2024nlp}, its
Gauss--Newton phase and its null-space view of the constraints, but not its
interior samplers, which we first tried and could not turn into usable
polytopes. Seeding IRIS regions with grasp samples produced many tiny
polytopes, because a region grown around a thin, curved set is bounded by its
curvature in every direction. Fitting a union of polytopes to the samples with
a small network, each neuron a half-space, a region the maximum over its
half-spaces and the set the minimum over regions, never fitted the grasp set
closely enough to plan with. Hit-and-run itself mixed poorly in the wrist and
cap coordinates, since its line search shares one step size across all of
them. What works instead is to exploit the structure of the constraints, above
all their exact invariance to the wrist, rather than to rediscover it from
samples. The price is locality: one seed yields one grasp set, valid near that
seed. The restarts of NLP sampling spread seeds across the components of the
grasp manifold, and are the natural way to obtain the several grasp sets the
search already accepts. \todo{numbers for the attempts, if
space allows.}

\paragraph{Scope}
Exact intervals rely on Assumption~\ref{ass:task}, which covers caps, knobs,
valves and cranks but not general pick-and-place. With more object
coordinates, the object configurations reachable in a set become sets that
could be propagated with Minkowski sums and intersections, as in
CASSR~\cite{wang2026cassr}, at the cost of exact dominance and with nonlinear
grasp transforms to approximate.

\paragraph{Optimality and offline cost}
The search minimises the number of grasps, then the number of set changes,
which is only a weak proxy for distance travelled. As in CASSR, the sets are
fixed before the configurations, so path length is not optimal. A new obstacle layout requires a new decomposition, which
dominates the offline cost: in a separate run, IRIS-NP2 took 120\,s for 25
regions at a 0.98 coverage target \tocheck{different settings}. GPU
implementations~\cite{werner2025superfast} reduce this cost, and new start
and goal queries reuse the graph unchanged.
