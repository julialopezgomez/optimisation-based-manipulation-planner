\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Tasks such as turning a cap, a valve or a knob require a robot to grasp the
same object many times, and the number of grasps depends on the goal, the
kinematics and the obstacles. We represented the manipulation graph with
convex sets, so that the switches between moving alone and moving the object
are convex transition sets, and searched them with an A* whose nodes pair a
set with the interval of object configurations reachable inside it. For
objects with one coordinate coupled to the wrist, this search is complete and
returns the minimum number of grasps relative to the sets, without fixing
that number in advance and without integer variables. It brings to
regrasping the idea, matured in footstep planning, of searching over convex
reachable sets rather than individual contacts, extended to contact sets that
must be constructed and to contacts that move the object. Its guarantees are
only as good as the transition sets near contact, which is why we validate
