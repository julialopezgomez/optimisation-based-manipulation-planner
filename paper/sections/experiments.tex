\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

Our experiments ask three questions. Does the search find minimum-grasp plans
whose length adapts to the query, for new goals, start configurations and
obstacle layouts? How does it compare with the MIQP and with sampling-based
manipulation planners as the number of required regrasps grows? How faithful
are the sets it reasons over?

\subsection{Tasks and implementation}
Both tasks turn a cap about a fixed vertical axis with a Franka
Panda~\cite{haddadin2022franka} whose wrist is restricted to $[-1,1]$\,rad, so
any turn larger than 2\,rad needs several grasps.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{T1 (3-DOF).} All arm joints but the wrist are locked:
    $q=(w,x_{\mathrm{finger}},\alpha)$ with $x_{\mathrm{finger}}\in[-0.045,0]$\,m
    and $\alpha\in[-\pi,\pi]$. The decomposition has 15 regions. The task
    turns the cap by $2\pi$.
  \item \textbf{T2 (10-DOF).} Seven arm joints, two fingers and the cap. The
    cap is 0.5\,m in front of the robot base, with two cylindrical obstacles
    (radius 5\,mm) next to it. The decomposition is an IRIS-NP2 clique cover
    with 42 regions \tocheck{coverage target 0.98; $(\varepsilon,\delta)$;
    generation time}. Grasp polytopes use~\eqref{eq:graspPoly} with
    $\epsilon=10^{-3}$, $\rho=0.05$\,rad, and $\Lmin=0.05$\,rad. The reference
    query Q0 turns the cap from 3 to $-1$\,rad and returns the arm to its
    start configuration.
\end{itemize}
The planner is implemented in Python on Drake~\cite{tedrake2019drake}
(v1.48). The LPs use Drake's \texttt{MathematicalProgram}, and the MIQP and the
GCS segments of the MIQP pipeline use MOSEK~\cite{mosek}.

\subsection{Protocol}
\label{sec:experiments:protocol}

\textbf{E1, goal sweep:} $\agoal$ on a grid over the cap range, with the arm
configuration fixed. We report the number of grasp actions against the lower
bound $2\lceil D/\Lmax\rceil$ and the search time. \textbf{E2, random
queries:} $N$ start and goal arm configurations sampled in the regions, with
random turning angles. \textbf{E3, obstacle layouts:} $M$ placements of the
obstacles, each with its own decomposition. We report offline time
(decomposition, grasp pieces, graph) and online time per query.
\textbf{E4, baselines:} the MIQP of Section~\ref{sec:method:miqp} and a
sampling-based manipulation planner on the manipulation
graph~\cite{simeon2004manipulation,mirabel2016hpp}, as a function of the
number of regrasps the query requires. Metrics are success within a time
limit, planning time, grasp actions and path length. For all methods, every
returned motion is checked densely with Drake's collision checker.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Reachability A* on the 10-DOF task}
Table~\ref{tab:franka} and Fig.~\ref{fig:reach} report query Q0. Of the 42
regions, 8 intersect the grasp polytope. Four of these grasp pieces span the
whole wrist range ($L_k=2$\,rad), and the others span between 0.06 and
0.36\,rad. The search expands 63 nodes in 4\,ms and returns two strokes, one
return and one release: enter $G_6$ from $F_2$, stroke, return, stroke,
release into $F_0$. Its cost, $4+\mu$, equals the initial heuristic plus one
set change, so the heuristic is tight on this query. With $D=4$\,rad and
$\Lmax=2$\,rad, two strokes is the minimum the decomposition allows. The
placement stage turns 2\,rad per stroke and produces 7 waypoints. All 6
straight-line motions lie in their certifying set and satisfy the coupling
rule. The planner never calls a collision checker. Its only geometric
operations are the LPs of the offline stage.

\subsection{Comparison with the MIQP}
Table~\ref{tab:miqp} compares the two sequencing methods. On T1, the MIQP
returns 11 grasps after proving 10 infeasible. Each stroke turns the cap by
0.604\,rad, the wrist range of the grasp set in that decomposition, and
$\lceil 2\pi/0.604\rceil=11$. GCS then connects all 23 transit and transfer
segments. On T2, the MIQP does not return a reliable solution, for the
reasons of Section~\ref{sec:method:miqp}, whereas the search returns the
plan above in milliseconds. \tocheck{MIQP outcome and solve time on T2; run

\subsection{How faithful are the sets near contact?}
\label{sec:results:fidelity}
A planner that is complete and optimal relative to a decomposition inherits
its errors. To measure them where they matter, we swept the wrist over
$[-1,1]$ at the seed grasp, at 99 points, and compared region membership with
Drake's collision checker. The checker finds the wrist free only on
$[-1,-0.19]$ and $[0.70,0.92]$\,rad \tocheck{which bodies collide in between: fingers vs.\ obstacles?}.
All four decompositions we computed for T2 (IRIS-NP2 with 42 and 46 regions, and two
IRIS-ZO covers with 64 regions \tocheck{the two IRIS-ZO files may be the same cover}) contain every point of the sweep. That gives
53 true positives, 46 false positives and no false negatives in each case. The
two-stroke plan of Fig.~\ref{fig:reach} is therefore valid relative to the
decomposition but collides in reality. With the true longest free segment of
0.81\,rad, the minimum is $\lceil4/0.81\rceil=5$ strokes, a different discrete
structure and not just a different geometry.

This is not a bug in the region generators, but a mismatch between their
guarantee and our use. IRIS-NP2 and IRIS-ZO bound the \emph{fraction of
volume} of a region in collision~\cite{werner2024faster}. A grasp piece is
almost a one-dimensional set of near-zero volume, so it can be entirely in
collision without violating that bound. Grasps also happen exactly where the
hand is closest to the obstacles. The same sensitivity appeared in
low-dimensional versions of the task~\cite{lopezgomez2025minf}. With a 4-DOF
gripper, a decomposition at 0.70 coverage contained no grasp--release
sequence, and at 0.90 it contained one that GCS could not connect. Coverage of
free space is the wrong measure for regrasp planning. What matters is
fidelity on the switch sets.
