\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Tasks}
All tasks are variants of a simplified bottle-cap opening task: a cap
rotates about its fixed vertical axis, and the goal is to turn it by a full
revolution, from $\qcap=-3.14$ to $\qcap=3.14$\,rad. The joint that rotates
the gripper about the cap axis has a limited range, so the task cannot be
completed with a single grasp. Since $\qcap$ is constant during transit
\eqref{eq:transit} and changes as much as $\qwr$ during transfer
\eqref{eq:transfer}, a range of 2\,rad requires at least four grasps.
\tocheck{verify against the plans found.} The task is deliberately simple
in its object motion, so that difficulty comes from regrasping, obstacles near
the contact, and the dimension of the composite space.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{T1, 3-DOF.} A simplified Schunk WSG gripper welded at a fixed
    distance from the cap, with a revolute joint about the cap axis,
    $\theta_{\mathrm{gr}}\in[-1,1]$\,rad, and one prismatic finger,
    $z_{\mathrm{finger}}\in[-0.055,0]$\,m; the composite configuration is
    $q=(\qcap,\theta_{\mathrm{gr}},z_{\mathrm{finger}})$. Two cylindrical
    obstacles are placed next to the cap. Its 3D composite space can be
    visualised directly.
  \item \textbf{T2, 4-DOF.} T1 with the second finger as an additional
    prismatic joint.
  \item \textbf{T3, Franka hand, 4-DOF.} \tocheck{keep?} A Franka Panda with
    all arm joints but the wrist locked, two fingers and the cap.
  \item \textbf{T4, Franka arm, 10-DOF.} A Franka Panda~\cite{haddadin2022franka}
    with its 7 arm joints and 2 finger joints, and the cap, placed $0.5$\,m in
    front of the robot base, with two cylindrical obstacles next to it
    (Fig.~\ref{fig:franka}). The wrist joint is restricted to $[-1,1]$\,rad.
    Grasps are defined by \eqref{eq:grasp_h}--\eqref{eq:grasp_g}.
    \todo{obstacle sizes; start/goal arm configurations.}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Implementation}
The planner is implemented in Python on top of
Drake~\cite{tedrake2019drake} (v1.48.0; later versions showed a regression
with the MOSEK interface), with MOSEK~\cite{mosek} solving the MIQP and the
GCS programs. The clique-cover decompositions use Drake's IRIS-NP
implementation. The NLP sampler is our Python port of the reference
implementation of~\cite{toussaint2024nlp}, with forward kinematics and
Jacobians from Drake's automatic differentiation. \todo{hardware; code

\subsection{Metrics}
We report success rate over \tbd{} queries and random seeds; offline time
(decomposition and grasp polytopes); online time (sequencing and GCS); number
of grasps; trajectory length in configuration space; and the maximum
violation of the grasp constraint $\|h\|_\infty$ along transfer segments, a
measure of how faithfully the polytopes represent the grasp manifold.

\subsection{Low-DOF tasks}
Table~\ref{tab:lowdof} reports the effect of the coverage of the
decomposition on the low-DOF tasks. \todo{These numbers are from the MInf
report / Year-1 annual review (earlier pipeline, no mode constraints inside
GCS). Re-run with the final pipeline, add T3, and average over seeds.} In
T1, even moderate coverage is enough to find a plan, but trajectory time grows
with the number of regions. In T2, low coverage is not enough: at a target of
$0.70$ no feasible grasp--release sequence exists in the decomposition, and at
$0.90$ a sequence is found but its transit and transfer segments cannot be
connected. Full plans are found from a target coverage of about $0.96$. This
is expected, as grasps lie close to contact, near the boundary of
$\CSfree$, and small gaps in the decomposition can remove exactly the regions
needed to regrasp. Trajectory time depends on the number and shape of the
regions as much as on the dimension: T1 at $0.99$ takes longer than T2 at
$0.96$.
