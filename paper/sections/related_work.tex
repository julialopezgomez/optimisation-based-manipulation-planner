\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Manipulation and multi-modal planning}
Manipulation planning extends motion planning with movable objects that can
only move when grasped~\cite{alami1990geometrical,latombe1991robot}. Early
methods assumed finite, user-given sets of grasps and placements, and searched
over the resulting discrete transitions~\cite{alami1990geometrical,tournassoud1987regrasping}.
Treating grasps and placements as continuous sets was a major step: in the
manipulation graph of Sim\'eon et al.~\cite{simeon2004manipulation}, nodes are
connected components of the intersection of grasp and placement spaces and
edges are transit or transfer paths, both computed with probabilistic
roadmaps. Multi-modal planners generalise this structure to sequences of
modes connected through their intersections~\cite{hauser2010multimodal,hauser2011randomized},
and asymptotically optimal variants exist~\cite{schmitt2017optimal,vegabrown2016asymptotically}.
Task and motion planning (TAMP) adds symbolic decisions on top of this
geometry~\cite{garrett2021integrated}, either by calling continuous samplers
from a symbolic planner~\cite{garrett2020pddlstream} or by optimising over a
fixed skeleton~\cite{toussaint2015lgp}. Constraint-based frameworks such as
HPP represent grasp, placement and motion constraints explicitly and explore
their connectivity with constrained solvers~\cite{mirabel2016hpp}. Our
planner keeps the manipulation-graph structure of~\cite{simeon2004manipulation},
but represents its nodes and edges with convex sets rather than samples, so
that the choice of grasps and the motions between them can be optimised.

\subsection{Convex decompositions of C-free and Graphs of Convex Sets}
Describing configuration-space obstacles exactly is
intractable~\cite{lozano1983spatial,canny1988complexity}, which motivates
describing $\CSfree$ directly as a union of simpler sets. IRIS grows large
convex regions in free space by alternating separating hyperplanes and
inscribed ellipsoids~\cite{deits2015computing}. C-IRIS certifies regions as
collision-free through a rational parametrisation of the kinematics and
sums-of-squares programs~\cite{amice2022finding,dai2024certified}, while
IRIS-NP, IRIS-NP2 and IRIS-ZO trade certification for speed and scale with
probabilistic guarantees~\cite{petersen2023growing,werner2024faster}.
Clique covers of visibility graphs automate seeding and cover $\CSfree$ with
few regions~\cite{werner2024approximating}, and recent GPU implementations make
decompositions fast enough for online use~\cite{werner2025superfast}. Convex
decompositions have long been used in mixed-integer
planning~\cite{schouwenaars2001mixed,deits2015efficient}, and also in
contact planning for legged robots~\cite{deits2014footstep,aceituno2018simultaneous,tonneau2020sl1m}.
GCS~\cite{marcucci2024shortest,marcucci2023motion} solves shortest-path
problems over such decompositions through a tight convex relaxation, and has
been extended to non-Euclidean joint spaces~\cite{cohn2023noneuclidean},
temporal-logic specifications~\cite{kurtz2023temporal}, contact-rich planar
manipulation~\cite{graesdal2024tight}, and heuristic search over implicitly
defined graphs (GCS*)~\cite{chia2024gcsstar}. Interleaving graph search with
convex or trajectory optimisation has also been explored
in~\cite{natarajan2021insat}. To our knowledge, these tools have not been used
to plan grasp and regrasp sequences for prehensile manipulation in the

\subsection{Constrained sampling}
Grasps and placements define lower-dimensional constraint manifolds. Task
space regions express pose constraints for sampling-based
planners~\cite{berenson2011tsr}, and manifold planners explore such
constraints through tangent-space steps followed by
projection~\cite{jaillet2013path,kingston2019exploring}; see~\cite{kingston2018sampling}
for a review. Since these methods expand continuously from known feasible
states, they struggle when the feasible set is disconnected. NLP
sampling~\cite{toussaint2024nlp} instead targets diverse samples from
densities restricted to nonlinear equality and inequality constraints, through
a restarting two-phase scheme: a Gauss--Newton phase that finds a feasible
point, and an interior phase (e.g., nonlinear hit-and-run or manifold RRT) that
explores the feasible set from it. We use this sampler to seed grasp
polytopes on the grasp manifold of a 7-DOF arm.
