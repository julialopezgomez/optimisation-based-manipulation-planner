\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Coverage near contact}
The low-DOF experiments show that the planner is only as reliable as the
decomposition it operates on. Uniform coverage of $\CSfree$ is not what
matters most: grasps happen near contact, at the boundary of $\CSfree$, and
missing a small region there can make an otherwise solvable task infeasible.
This suggests biasing the decomposition towards task-relevant sets, e.g., by
seeding region growth with samples on the grasp and placement manifolds rather
than uniformly. \todo{revisit once 10-DOF coverage results are in.}

\paragraph{Representing grasp manifolds with polytopes}
Before the null-space construction of Section~\ref{sec:method:grasp}, we
explored two alternatives on T4. First, we used NLP samples of the grasp
constraints as seeds for IRIS-type region growing. This produced a very large
number of very small polytopes, since regions grown around a thin curved
manifold are limited by its curvature in every direction, which made
sequencing slow and fragmented. Second, we fitted a union of polytopes to the
NLP samples with a small max--min network, in which each neuron is a
half-space $a_k^\top q-b_k$, a region is the maximum over its half-spaces and
the set is the minimum over regions, with unused half-spaces pruned. This
learns all polytopes at once, but did not yet represent the grasp set
accurately enough to plan with. \todo{one figure or numbers for each, if
space allows.} The null-space construction works because it uses the
structure of the constraint, in particular its exact invariance to wrist and
cap rotation, instead of discovering it from samples. Its cost is that each
polytope is thin, which affects the numerics of the MIQP
(Section~\ref{sec:method:sequencing}).

\paragraph{Sampling in the tangent configuration space}
Since C-IRIS certifies regions in the stereographic coordinates
$t=\tan(\theta/2)$, where forward kinematics is rational, we also tested
whether NLP sampling mixes better in these coordinates. It did not on a 2-DOF
sub-problem of the Panda with a sphere obstacle: joints whose range lies near
$\pm\pi$ are stretched by the map (e.g., the fourth Panda joint,
$[-3.07,-0.07]$\,rad, maps to roughly $[-28.6,-0.03]$), so a sampler with a
single step size barely moves along them. Per-coordinate step sizes would be
needed. \tocheck{keep as a short remark or drop; preliminary, 2-DOF only.}

\paragraph{Limitations}
The method addresses prehensile manipulation of a single rigid object with
known geometry, quasi-statically. Linear manipulation constraints
\eqref{eq:transit}--\eqref{eq:transfer} follow from the symmetry of the cap
task; general objects with $SE(3)$ placements make the transfer constraint
nonlinear, and would require either polytopes in grasp-relative coordinates or
constraints enforced approximately. The grasp polytopes are validated
empirically rather than certified, and the decomposition is collision-free
only with high probability unless certified. GCS returns solutions that are
optimal relative to the decomposition only when its convex relaxation is
tight~\cite{marcucci2024shortest}, and the keyframes are chosen with a
surrogate cost. Finally, the offline decomposition is expensive in high
dimension and must be recomputed when the scene changes, although GPU
implementations~\cite{werner2025superfast} reduce this cost.

regions are grown directly on the grasp manifold (we have ported IRIS-ZO and
clique covers to Python to make the particle sampler replaceable), richer
contact modes such as pushing, pivoting and in-hand manipulation, and
experiments on the real robot. \todo{trim to what fits.}
