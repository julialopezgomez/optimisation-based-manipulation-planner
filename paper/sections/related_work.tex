\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Regrasp and manipulation planning}
The first regrasp planners searched over finite sets of grasps and
placements~\cite{tournassoud1987regrasping,alami1990geometrical}. The
idea persists in regrasp graphs built over thousands of precomputed
grasps~\cite{wan2017regrasp}, and in a bimanual planner certified complete
with respect to its discretisation~\cite{lertkultanon2018certified}.
Such planners are complete only with respect to the grasps they enumerate: a
solution that needs a grasp between two discretised ones is missed. The
manipulation graph~\cite{simeon2004manipulation} made grasps and placements
continuous. It was extended to multi-modal
planning~\cite{hauser2010multimodal}, to asymptotically
optimal sampling~\cite{schmitt2017optimal}, and to
constraint graphs explored with constrained
samplers~\cite{mirabel2016hpp,kingston2018sampling}. Their guarantees are asymptotic: they hold in the
limit of infinitely many samples. Task and motion planners add symbolic decisions on
top~\cite{garrett2021integrated}. In optimisation-based
TAMP, an outer search enumerates skeletons with a fixed number of mode
switches, and a local solver handles each one~\cite{toussaint2015lgp}.
Closest in spirit, Liu et al.~\cite{liu2026graph} plan contact-rich planar
manipulation on a graph of reachable sets of object poses
\tocheck{characterisation}. Our transition sets are the nodes of the
manipulation graph of~\cite{simeon2004manipulation}, built as convex sets
rather than sampled. This is what lets us guarantee completeness and the
minimum number of grasps after a finite computation, relative to the sets.

\subsection{Contact planning over convex sets}
Footstep planning faces the same combinatorics as regrasping: a contact
sequence whose length is not known in advance. A* over discretised footholds~\cite{griffin2019footstep}
is complete only at the resolution of the discretisation. Mixed-integer
programs over convex
terrain~\cite{deits2014footstep} avoid
discretisation and are globally optimal, but the number of steps, or a bound
on it, must be fixed, and their solve times are hard to bound. $\ell_1$
relaxations~\cite{tonneau2020sl1m} make them fast at the cost
of these guarantees. \tocheck{SL1M characterisation.} CASSR~\cite{wang2026cassr} instead searches with A* over
convex sets of footholds reachable from the start, expanded by Minkowski sums,
and places the footsteps with a quadratic program once the sets are chosen.
CASSR discretises the yaw of the feet, and inflates its heuristic for speed,
so its optimality is empirical rather than guaranteed. We follow the same principle, but a node's
reachable set carries the configuration of the object rather than a foothold.
In the coupled tasks we consider it is an interval, which lets us keep an
admissible heuristic and exact pruning, with nothing discretised.

Graphs of Convex Sets (GCS)~\cite{marcucci2024shortest,marcucci2023motion}
compute shortest paths over convex decompositions through a convex relaxation,
and have been extended to contact-rich planar
pushing~\cite{graesdal2024tight}. They plan the motion of a robot in one mode,
with no switches of grasp and no object state carried from one switch to the
next. A GCS path also visits each set at most once, so a plan that comes back
to the same set, as every regrasp does, needs the graph unrolled once per
grasp, with the number of grasps bounded in advance
(Section~\ref{sec:results:gcs}). GCS*~\cite{chia2024gcsstar} searches such graphs heuristically, and is
complete and optimal when it can decide exactly whether one partial path
dominates another; in general that check must be approximated. With interval
reachable sets, it is exact.

\subsection{Convex decompositions of free space}
Convex decompositions inner-approximate free space with convex sets. IRIS
grows them by alternating separating hyperplanes and inscribed
ellipsoids~\cite{deits2015computing}, and C-IRIS certifies them collision-free
with sums-of-squares programs~\cite{dai2024certified}. IRIS-NP, IRIS-NP2 and
IRIS-ZO scale further under a probabilistic guarantee: the fraction of a
region's volume in collision is below a threshold, with a given
confidence~\cite{petersen2023growing,werner2024faster}. Clique covers automate
the seeding~\cite{werner2024approximating}, and GPU implementations approach
online rates~\cite{werner2025superfast}. A volume guarantee says nothing
about sets of near-zero volume, such as grasp sets. This is why we validate
the transition sets separately (Section~\ref{sec:method:graph}).
