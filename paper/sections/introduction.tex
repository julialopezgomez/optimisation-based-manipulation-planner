\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Manipulating an object involves more than moving a robot without collision.
The robot must decide where to grasp the object, how to carry it, where to
release it and, when its own kinematics prevent the task from being completed
in one motion, where to regrasp it. Geometrically, this is a motion planning
problem in the \emph{composite} configuration space of robot and
object~\cite{alami1990geometrical,simeon2004manipulation}, where the object can
only move while it is grasped. Valid solutions therefore alternate between
\emph{transit} paths, in which the robot moves alone while the object rests at
a stable placement, and \emph{transfer} paths, in which the robot moves while
holding the object at a fixed grasp. Switching between the two is only
possible on the intersection of the grasp and placement spaces, a set of
measure zero in the composite space. Together with collision avoidance and
joint limits, this makes the feasible space narrow, disconnected and highly
nonlinear.

The two dominant families of planners handle this structure in complementary
ways. Sampling-based planners build roadmaps or trees over sampled
configurations~\cite{kavraki1996prm,lavalle1998rrt}, and their extension to
manipulation through the manipulation graph~\cite{simeon2004manipulation} and
multi-modal planning~\cite{hauser2010multimodal} gives probabilistic
completeness and, for some variants, asymptotic
optimality~\cite{karaman2011sampling,schmitt2017optimal}. In practice,
however, they degrade with dimension and in narrow passages, and require
dedicated samplers to hit the lower-dimensional sets where grasps happen.
Trajectory optimisation~\cite{zucker2013chomp,schulman2014motion} instead
exploits gradients and optimises task costs directly, but collision and grasp
constraints are non-convex, so solutions are local, depend on initialisation,
and failure gives no information about whether a solution exists.

A third route has matured in recent years. The collision-free configuration
space $\CSfree$ can be approximated as a union of convex
polytopes~\cite{deits2015computing,amice2022finding,dai2024certified,petersen2023growing,werner2024faster},
with seeding automated through clique covers of visibility
graphs~\cite{werner2024approximating}. On such a decomposition, Graphs of
Convex Sets (GCS)~\cite{marcucci2023motion,marcucci2024shortest} compute
trajectories that are globally optimal \emph{relative to the decomposition}
by solving a tight convex relaxation of a mixed-integer program. These tools
turn $\CSfree$ into an object that an optimiser can reason about directly, but
they have so far mainly been applied to collision-free motion of the robot
alone. \tocheck{Check whether any recent work applies C-free decompositions + GCS
to prehensile regrasp planning; closest known: contact-rich planar

In this paper, we bring convex decompositions to prehensile manipulation
planning. We decompose the collision-free composite configuration space,
describe the grasp and placement spaces as unions of polytopes, and intersect
them with the decomposition. The nodes of the classical manipulation graph,
i.e., the connected components of grasp-and-placement configurations, then
become unions of convex sets over which we can optimise. A minimum-regrasp
sequence of grasp and release configurations is selected by a mixed-integer
quadratic program (MIQP) or by A* search over the polytope
graph~\cite{hart1968astar}, and connected by optimal transit and transfer
trajectories computed with GCS (Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}). A preliminary
version of this pipeline was demonstrated on 3-DOF tasks
in~\cite{lopezgomez2025minf}. \tocheck{anonymise if the venue is
double-blind.}

Scaling this idea to a full arm raises a problem that does not appear in
low-dimensional examples. For a gripper with a few joints, grasp and placement
spaces can be written directly as bounds on the configuration. For a 7-DOF arm,
a grasp is a nonlinear constraint on the forward kinematics, and the set of
grasping configurations is a thin, curved, lower-dimensional manifold. To
represent it with polytopes, we use NLP
sampling~\cite{toussaint2024nlp}, a restarting two-phase constrained sampler,
to generate diverse grasp configurations, and construct around each one a
polytope aligned with the null space of the active grasp constraints.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A formulation of prehensile manipulation planning over convex
    decompositions of the collision-free composite configuration space, in
    which the grasp, placement and transition sets of the manipulation graph
    are represented as unions of convex polytopes
    (Sections~\ref{sec:problem} and~\ref{sec:method:graph}).
  \item A two-stage planner that selects a minimum-regrasp sequence of
    grasp and release configurations, by MIQP or by A* search over the
    polytope graph, and connects it with GCS transit and transfer
    trajectories that are optimal relative to the decomposition
    (Sections~\ref{sec:method:sequencing}--\ref{sec:method:gcs}).
  \item A construction of grasp polytopes for high-DOF arms, combining NLP
    sampling of the nonlinear grasp constraints with null-space-aligned
    polytopes around the samples (Section~\ref{sec:method:grasp}).
  \item An evaluation on bottle-cap opening tasks requiring repeated
    regrasping, from 3-DOF and 4-DOF systems to the 10-dimensional composite
    space of a Franka Panda arm, hand and cap, with a comparison against
    sampling-based planners (Sections~\ref{sec:experiments}--\ref{sec:results}).

\end{itemize}
