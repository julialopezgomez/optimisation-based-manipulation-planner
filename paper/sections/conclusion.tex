\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
A manipulation plan is a sequence of contacts, and separating the choice of
contacts from the motions that realise them is what costs feasibility and
optimality. We kept the two together by building the manipulation graph out of
convex sets and searching over reachability: a node pairs a convex set with
the interval of object configurations reachable inside it. For objects with
one coordinate coupled to the wrist, the search is complete and returns the
fewest grasps the sets allow, without fixing that number in advance and
without integer variables, and a solver-free stage recovers the motions. This
carries over to regrasping an idea matured in footstep planning, and extends
it to contact sets that must be constructed and to contacts that move the
object. The guarantees are only as good as the transition sets are faithful
