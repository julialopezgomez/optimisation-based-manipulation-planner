What is compared

  • Reachability A* (ours). Search over (convex set, reachable cap-angle interval) nodes; cost = grasps + ungrasps, plus 10−3 per set change. Second stage: solver-free placement, then one SOCP that shortens the path over the same sets.
  • Full GCS. The same sets and edges, copied once per grasp (GCS visits each vertex at most once), cost = length + 10 per grasp. Drake's SolvePath: convex relaxation, rounding to ≤10 paths, MOSEK.
  • Manipulation RRT (HPP). HPP's constraint graph on the same robot and scene, continuous path validation, paths not shortcut. 10 seeds per query (1 cap) or 3 (8 caps), 60 s each.
  • Every returned plan is re-checked with Drake: collisions, the coupling (the cap turns only with the grasping wrist) and the goal.

Offline stage

Done once per scene and shared by the A* and GCS; HPP needs none. C-free is 9-D (arm + fingers, caps welded, which is exact), seeded along each grasp's wrist runs, and its false positives on those wrist sweeps are cut off.

  • The A* returns the lower bound on every turn. Full GCS uses one grasp more at D = 1.5, 2.4 and 6 (red), although the A*'s plan is a path in the same GCS graph with a lower GCS cost (e.g. 36.0 vs 44.0 at D = 1.5): rounding the relaxation is not optimal.
  • GCS on the A* sequence (the convex restriction of the same layered GCS graph to the A*'s sets) returns the A*'s lengths to seven digits: the A*'s second stage is that same convex program, so it is optimal for the sets the A* chose. Its time includes the A* search and building the layered graph.
  • Full GCS finds shorter paths: 19% and 10% shorter at the two turns with equal grasps (D = 0.5, 3.2). Since the second stage is already optimal for the A*'s sets, the gap comes from which sets the A* picks (by number of set changes, not length).
  • HPP solves only D = 0.5 (7/10 runs, 18% longer than the A*). Nothing with two or more grasps, at 60 s, nor at 600 s on the scene without the floor (0/10 at D = 1.5 and 6).

Notes

  • An earlier decomposition of the one-cap scene (42 regions, clique cover only, 10-D) called colliding wrist configurations free, and full GCS on it took 44–93 s and 4–6 GB, and did not fit in 60 GB at 8 grasps. Those runs are in the paper's appendix.
  • The A* graph's LPs use quadprog (regularised LP, no licence); with MOSEK each LP checked a licence out, and the 8-cap graph took 15 min.
  • Open: adding path length to the A*'s cost (a door point per node, λ‖p − pgoal‖ in the heuristic) would target the 10–19% length gap at the price of more nodes.
